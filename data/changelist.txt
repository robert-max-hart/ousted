# split by vertical bars, then "- " bullets. The newest version is the build id on the title screen.

# 0.6.1-dev | 30 Oct 2026 | M7 begins: moving water and trees and grass that sway in the wind
- The water is now a shader: gentle swells, ripples that catch the sun and moon, and a broken foam edge along the shore. It is a clearer blue than before, and stops when the game is paused.
- Pines, oaks, bushes, hazel and the grass sway in the wind, more at the top than the base, out of step with their neighbours. The wind picks up in rain and the sway stops when the game is paused.

# 0.6.0 | 30 Oct 2026 | Meta is complete: hills, overwrite confirmation and a steadier fishing catch
- This build closes the Meta milestone: save slots, settings, map size, water layouts, hills, climates, custom rules, realism mode, start options, the slope overlay, a tutorial and scenarios. The Arctic climate is still flagged as not yet tuned.
- New Game has a "Hills" choice (Flat, Few, Normal, Many): how many hill regions the map has and how high they rise. Flat has almost no steep ground, so stone and iron are scarce beyond the start; Many is hilly and rich in both but has little level ground. Saves remember it.
- Saving over an existing save now asks first: the Save button turns into Overwrite. The save, settings and scenario windows are opaque, so the menu no longer shows through them.
- Fishing huts catch more on a river: the catch grows with the square root of the water share instead of the share itself.

# 0.5.1 | 30 Oct 2026 | Meta, part 1: save slots, settings, new map and game options, a tutorial and scenarios
- The Esc menu has "Save game..." and "Load game...": save under any name, pick from a list of saves (newest first, with the date and citizen count) and delete old ones. F5 and F9 still quicksave and quickload.
- The title screen has a "Load Game" button that opens the same list.
- New Settings screen (title and Esc menu): grass tufts (off, low, normal, dense), shadows, fullscreen and vertical sync. Saved between runs.
- New Game lets you pick the map size (Small 96, Medium 128, Large 160, Huge 192 cells a side) and the river: none, gentle, winding, wild or wide. Saves remember both; older saves load as a Large map with the winding river.
- The River choice on New Game is now "Water" and has two new layouts: Wetlands (most of the map is shallow, wadeable water with islands, and a dry rise to start on) and Coast (one side of the map is open ocean with a wandering shoreline).
- New Game has a Realism mode (days last twice as long in real time, and citizens age at the pace of the calendar: a citizen year is a game year) and a Custom rules block: starting settlers (6 to 20), starting supplies (50% to 200%), merchant visits (none to frequent) and droughts on or off. The New Game panel scrolls on small screens, and its Start button stays in view.
- New Scenarios button on the title screen: five preset games with goals (Riverside Beginnings, Dry Season, The Wetlands, The Coast, Bare Hands). Each has its own fixed map, so everyone plays the same one, with a check box to draw a random map instead. A goals panel in the game ticks goals off as you reach them, a victory message comes when all are done (you can keep playing), and completed scenarios are marked in the list.
- The Load Game, Settings and Scenarios windows now draw over the title menu.
- New Tutorial button on the title screen: a guided first game (easy start, medium map, no droughts) with a panel that walks through 15 steps (look around, settle, time, wood, a house, roads, a field, overlays, jobs, firewood, saving) and moves on by itself as you do each thing. Any step can be skipped and the tutorial ended at any time.
- Custom rules has a new "Start with" choice: Town Hall (as before), Well (only a well) or Nothing (no buildings at all). Whatever does not fit in the Town Hall, or all of it without one, starts in supply carts, and you place the first building (or the cart) when you choose where to settle. A Town Hall can still be built later.
- New top bar button "Slope" (beside Soil and Water, only one overlay at a time) colours the ground by how steep it is for building: green is level, yellow and orange need levelling, red is too steep.
- New Game also picks a climate: Temperate (as before), Arctic (short cool summers, brutal winters), Desert (hot, dry, droughts, thin woods) or Tropical (warm all year with frequent rain, thick woods, fast spoilage). Hot climates show no winter look.

# 0.5.0 | 30 Oct 2026 | Trade is complete: one-tile bridges, a fishing pier and playtest 4 fixes
- This build closes the trade milestone: merchants and merchant types, barter, luxury goods, seeds and animals for sale, a trade history, rivers, bridges and fishing.
- Crop fields now cost wood and graveyards cost stone (the cost grows with the length of their fence or wall).
- Day and night now blend: the sun fades out as the moon fades in (two lights that never swap places), so dusk and dawn no longer jump.
- The Fishing Hut now has a pier: the door faces the shore and the pier at the back must run out over the water (turn it with R). Fishermen walk out and work at the end of the pier. The ghost's red note says why a spot is refused.
- The Harvest tool has Trees, Stone and Iron check boxes, so you can mark only some kinds in an area.
- Young trees hold no wood: a tree gives nothing until it is about a third grown, and most of its wood arrives late, so planting a tree and chopping it at once no longer works.
- New Water overlay (top bar, beside Soil): blue-green ground is within reach of water (open water, a well or the Town Hall), where a Distillery or Potter's Workshop works; brown ground is too far.
- Five new orchard trees: cherry, peach and plum (fruit, which the Distillery can turn into spirits) and walnut and pecan (nuts, which keep a very long time and cannot be distilled).
- Seeds and saplings cost ten times as much from merchants.
- Clicking the merchant banner under the top bar now really takes the camera to the merchant (it did nothing before).
- Rain and snow dim the daylight much more, so wet days no longer look bright.
- River banks are gentler (easier to place a fishing hut) and a ford has a hand of water over it.
- Hold Shift while dragging a road for a straight diagonal line.
- The marks for single trees can be clicked and cancelled one by one.
- The Goods held for trade window has +25 and -25 buttons.
- The top and bottom bars have square corners where they meet the screen edge.
- Every building now stays inside its footprint (anvils, racks and porches no longer reach onto a road beside it): buildings that stuck out are slightly smaller.
- The title screen's town has a market stall, an apiary, a tailor, a still, a potter, a pasture, an orchard, and a fishing hut and bridge on the river.
- Bridges are one tile wide, like roads: press at one end of the crossing and drag along it (up to 16 long). They cost 1 wood a tile.
- While you drag a stockpile, field, graveyard, pasture, orchard or bridge, the note by the cursor now shows the cost as well as the size.

# 0.4.5 | 30 Oct 2026 | Rivers, bridges and playtest fixes
- Every map has a winding river from one edge to the other, wider downstream. Citizens can wade through its shallow fords (at half speed); deep water still blocks them. The land beside it is richer soil, and you start a short walk from it.
- New Bridge (Civic): drag a wooden deck at least 2 cells wide across the water, with its ends on land. Citizens walk over it at full speed.
- Idle laborers now chop mature trees when the colony has under 30 wood, and unmarked gathering stays within 45 cells of the citizen (marked trees and rocks are still worked anywhere).
- Iron: the ring 14 to 30 cells from the start always has at least 2 outcrops. The start site is chosen for a wider area of level ground.
- Trade window redone: a table of price and stock for each side, +1 +5 -1 -5 zero and Max buttons, prices coloured green or red by how they favour you, goods the merchant asked for sorted to the top in bold on gold, and a bar comparing what they pay with what you owe.
- New: a Build another button, a student list at the School, a button to send a merchant away early, a click on the merchant banner to look at them, a front arrow on the build ghost, and a Game saved message.
- Woodcutters plant trees closer together and keep up to 60 saplings growing.
- Citizens freeze in their pose when the game is paused; scarecrows stand between plots; grass tufts are smaller and denser; road colour varies less; day and night blend more slowly; the Road tool is under Civic and the terrain brushes are gone from the toolbar.
- New Fishing Hut (Food) and Fisherman profession: build it on the shore with part of it over the water. Fishermen catch fish from the water in its circle: the more of the circle is water, the better the catch, and two huts whose circles overlap both catch less. Fish are a new food that spoils in 4 days.
- New games let you choose where to settle: look over the map, then click to place the Town Hall, and the settlers arrive there. A note beside the cursor rates the spot: level ground, water, soil, trees, wild food, stone and iron. Untick "Choose where to settle" on the New Game screen to have the game pick a spot.
- Saves from before this build keep their old map (no river).

# 0.4.3 | 30 Oct 2026 | Luxuries, clothing, merchant types and a darker palette
- New Apiary (Food): beehives that make honey in warm weather, more of it the more berry bushes, hazel groves and orchard trees bloom within its radius. Farmers collect it; it never spoils and is worth a lot to merchants, who bring and ask for it.
- Citizens eat honey now and then: a little food and a happiness boost that lasts four days. They only want another once it has worn off (and after a random pause), so a stock lasts. A new "Citizens enjoy treats" policy switches it off, to keep honey for trading.
- The Trading Post has an inventory: a "Goods held for trade" window sets how much of each item to keep at the post (up to 120 in all). Laborers carry the goods in from storage, and back out when you lower a level. Goods at the post are set aside: nobody can eat or burn them. An order uses the held goods first, so a prepared order completes at once; goods left by a merchant who departs stay at the post.
- One Pasture building for every animal: you choose chickens, sheep or cows in the inspector (changeable while it is empty). Old saves with a Chicken Run, Cow Pasture or Sheep Pasture load as a Pasture of that animal. Sheep (merchants sell them) grow wool through the year; farmers shear and collect it.
- New games start with random orchard saplings: two on Easy, one on Normal, none on Hard.
- Clothing: a Tailor's Workshop and a new Artisan profession. Artisans make wool clothes (3 wool), leather clothes (2 leather) and fine clothes (both, twice the work). Citizens put on the best clothes in storage; clothes cut how fast they lose warmth in the cold (30%, fine ones 60%) and wear out after 2.5 years (fine: 4.5), then get replaced from storage.
- Pastures have a "keep at most" setting: a herd over it has the surplus slaughtered for meat (food) and leather, which farmers collect. Wool, leather, meat and clothes all trade.
- A Distillery (Industry): an Artisan turns barley, apples or pears (4, with a little firewood) into 2 spirits. It only distils while the colony's food stock is over a level you set, so it cannot starve the colony. Citizens drink spirits now and then, like honey but for a bigger happiness boost (and they prefer them); merchants pay well for them.
- A Potter's Workshop (Industry): an Artisan turns 4 stone into 2 pieces of pottery, a good trade good.
- Buildings can now need water nearby: the Distillery and the Potter's Workshop only work with a well, the Town Hall or open water within 12 cells. The inspector says whether they have it.
- Silver and gold: a new Mine upgrade, Deep Veins (after Deep Shafts), lets miners dig silver (8 hours a unit) and gold (16), each up to a cap you set (defaults 10 and 6) after the other ores and before stone. They are only trade goods, worth a great deal (gold 80, silver 30), and merchants bring and ask for them.
- Four kinds of merchant, each with its own goods and cart colours: grain merchants (food, seeds and saplings, sometimes animals), metal traders (iron, coal, tools, silver and gold), herders (wool, leather, meat, eggs, and animals) and fine goods caravans (honey, spirits, pottery, fine clothes, precious metals). They call equally often in every season, and nobody asks for what they are selling.
- Luxuries (honey, spirits, pottery, fine clothes, silver, gold) fetch 1.4 to 2.2 times their value from any merchant who asks for them.
- Seasonal prices: grain and fruit are dear in spring and cheap at harvest, firewood, coal, clothes and spirits dear in winter, and so on. What is on offer does not change; only the price, fixed for the length of a visit. The barter window marks goods [dear] or [cheap].
- The Ledger has a Trade graph and a Trade tab: totals, every deal (who, what, the value each way) and every visit.
- An empty pasture now has room for any animal a merchant offers, and takes up the animal led to it. Animals waiting at the Trading Post with no pasture room are warned of in the log and the inspector.
- The Trading Post can keep honey and spirits back for trade: citizens only drink what is over the level you set.
- Every merchant also brings a small amount of one off-line item (firewood, manure, anything tradeable).
- A note under the top bar shows a merchant on the way, here (with the days left) or leaving; a warning in the log when a merchant is about to leave with an order unfinished; the barter window shows the usual price beside a dear or cheap one and is wider.
- Greener, deeper grass and calmer sand and spring oak leaves, and slightly softer daytime light, so the terrain looks less washed out.

# 0.4.2 | 28 Oct 2026 | Trade: merchants, barter, seeds and animals (M6 steps 1 to 3)

# 0.4.0 | 28 Oct 2026 | Agriculture depth: M4 complete (steps 4 to 8)
- Soil view: a Soil button in the top bar colours the ground by fertility (brown poor, green rich), numbers each plot of the selected field, and the crop list warns when a crop would drain poor soil or points out ones that restore it.
- Water: random droughts (no rain, hotter, everything dries fast), buckets (farmers fetch water from a well for dry plots), and irrigation channels dug from a river or lake that keep nearby plots moist.
- Pests: outbreaks that spread between plots, likelier when the same crop grows in several fields nearby; farmers pick them off, a frost clears them, and a Scarecrow field upgrade makes them rare.
- Orchards: apple and pear trees, planted once and picked every year. Saplings are found by foragers, and no orchard can be placed until one is found.
- Livestock: a Chicken Run (eggs) and a Cow Pasture (milk). Animals graze in warm weather, need grain in the cold months, breed up to the room, and starve without feed.
- Manure: animals leave it, farmers collect and spread it on poor fallow plots before sowing.
- The title screen has a Changelist button (this list) and shows the build id in the lower right.
