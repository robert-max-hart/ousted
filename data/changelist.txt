# 0.6.11 | 31 Oct 2026 | Graphics settings for trees and crops, and a fairer mix of trees
- Settings has two new options for weaker graphics cards. **Tree and crop distance** (Near, Normal, Far) stops drawing trees, bushes, rocks and crops past a set distance from the camera; Far, the default, draws everything as before. **Model detail** (Full, Reduced) swaps trees, bushes, rocks, orchard trees and crops for simpler models with fewer sides, leaves and stalks, about 40% fewer triangles for trees, bushes and rocks.
- The map's trees, bushes and rocks are now drawn in blocks, so the parts of the map that are off-screen are no longer drawn at all. This helps at every setting.
- Temperate forests now have the mix they were meant to have: pines, oaks and birches in similar shares. Birches had been far too common (about 40% of all trees) and they are lighter now too.

# 0.6.10 | 31 Oct 2026 | Fences, bridges and headstones with real shapes
- Fences are built from modelled pieces: wooden posts with capped tops and rails with nail heads, low field fences, rope posts with a drooping rope, and a graveyard wall of stacked stone blocks with piers. The gate gap now has a taller gatepost at each end (with a ball finial on the stone ones).
- Bridges have planked decks on stringers, handrails with capped posts along both sides, and stilts down into the water every other cell, however long you drag them and whichever way they run.
- The graveyard's headstones sit on little mounds of earth: a slab with cut lines of inscription, a round-topped stone with a bunch of flowers, and a cross on a stepped base.

# 0.6.9 | 31 Oct 2026 | Animals that move: pecking chickens, grazing sheep and cows, and the merchant's donkey
- The farm animals have real bodies: chickens with a comb, wattle, beak and tail feathers; sheep with a cloud of fleece, a dark face and ears; cows with patches, an udder, horns and a tail with a tuft. Each herd member has its own coat.
- They move properly: their legs swing as they walk (a chicken's alternate, a sheep's and cow's in diagonal pairs), they walk for a while and then stand with their heads down grazing or pecking, and they face the way they are heading (they used to walk backwards). They stop when the game is paused.
- The merchant's donkey is a proper donkey with long ears, a tail, a saddle cloth and a pack on each side, with four walking legs. Animals being led to a pasture are the same models.

# 0.6.7 | 31 Oct 2026 | Crops that look like crops, growing through the season

# 0.6.5 | 31 Oct 2026 | New trees: three kinds of pine, oak and birch, palms, dead trees, saplings, snow on the boughs, and trees that fall
- The forest has more variety: three shapes each of pine and oak, birches with white bark and a light crown, palms with arching fronds, and bare dead trees. A tree's shape is fixed by where it stands, so a forest never reshuffles.
- Each climate grows its own trees: temperate has pines, oaks and birches where they meet; arctic mostly pines and birches with a few dead trees; desert palms and many dead trees; tropical palms and broadleaf trees. Woodcutters replant what suits the climate.
- Young trees are drawn as real saplings and grow into the full tree in two stages instead of just getting bigger. Birch leaves turn yellow in autumn, a little before the oaks.
- Snow settles on the boughs of pines and on bushes in winter, following the snow on the ground.
- A tree cut down now tips over away from the woodcutter with a creak and a thump, lies a moment and sinks away, instead of vanishing.

# 0.6.1 | 31 Oct 2026 | M7 begins: moving water, swaying trees and grass, real snow, a new interface look, icons and graphics settings
- The water is now a shader: gentle swells, ripples that catch the sun and moon, and a broken foam edge along the shore. It is lighter and clearer than before, with the bed showing through, and stops when the game is paused.
- A richer colour grade over the whole picture (more saturation and contrast), so greens, roofs and shadows are less washed out, especially at golden hour. The hill rock is a little warmer.
- The ground has a shader: grain that breaks up flat colour, bands on steep rock, and snow cover that builds up while it snows and melts in warm weather, instead of everything turning white on the first day of winter. Snow settles on up-facing ground and the heights first, in patches at first, and lies thinly on roads and fields. Winter ground without snow is dry and brown.
- Pines, oaks, bushes, hazel and the grass sway in the wind, more at the top than the base, out of step with their neighbours. The wind picks up in rain and the sway stops when the game is paused.
- Settings now has a switch for each of the new effects, for weaker machines: water detail (Simple, Medium, Full), tree sway, grass sway, ground detail and snow edge detail.
- A new look for the interface: dark warm-brown panels with a thin gold edge, buttons that warm up under the cursor and turn gold when selected, gold-ticked check boxes, matching scroll bars, menus and tooltips, shared by the game screens, the pop-up windows and the title screen's windows.
- Small icons on the tool, build category and panel buttons, and beside each profession in the Professions list. The Soil, Water and Slope overlay buttons are icon-only to save room in the top bar (hover for the name).
- The icons are now image files (SVG) in `assets/icons/`, so they can be edited or replaced without touching code; a PNG with the same name takes precedence.
- Every building button in the build menu (and Road) now has its own icon, drawn as an SVG file in `assets/icons/buildings`.

# 0.4.3 | 30 Oct 2026 | Luxuries, clothing, merchant types and a darker palette
- New Apiary (Food): beehives that make honey in warm weather, more of it the more berry bushes, hazel groves and orchard trees bloom within its radius. Farmers collect it; it never spoils and is worth a lot to merchants, who bring and ask for it.
- Citizens eat honey now and then: a little food and a happiness boost that lasts four days. They only want another once it has worn off (and after a random pause), so a stock lasts. A new "Citizens enjoy treats" policy switches it off, to keep honey for trading.
- The Trading Post has an inventory: a "Goods held for trade" window sets how much of each item to keep at the post (up to 120 in all). Laborers carry the goods in from storage, and back out when you lower a level. Goods at the post are set aside: nobody can eat or burn them. An order uses the held goods first, so a prepared order completes at once; goods left by a merchant who departs stay at the post.
- One Pasture building for every animal: you choose chickens, sheep or cows in the inspector (changeable while it is empty). Old saves with a Chicken Run, Cow Pasture or Sheep Pasture load as a Pasture of that animal. Sheep (merchants sell them) grow wool through the year; farmers shear and collect it.
- New games start with random orchard saplings: two on Easy, one on Normal, none on Hard.
- Clothing: a Tailor's Workshop and a new Artisan profession. Artisans make wool clothes (3 wool), leather clothes (2 leather) and fine clothes (both, twice the work). Citizens put on the best clothes in storage; clothes cut how fast they lose warmth in the cold (30%, fine ones 60%) and wear out after 2.5 years (fine: 4.5), then get replaced from storage.
- Pastures have a "keep at most" setting: a herd over it has the surplus slaughtered for meat (food) and leather, which farmers collect. Wool, leather, meat and clothes all trade.
- A Distillery (Industry): an Artisan turns barley, apples or pears (4, with a little firewood) into 2 spirits. It only distils while the colony's food stock is over a level you set, so it cannot starve the colony. Citizens drink spirits now and then, like honey but for a bigger happiness boost (and they prefer them); merchants pay well for them.
- A Potter's Workshop (Industry): an Artisan turns 4 stone into 2 pieces of pottery, a good trade good.
- Buildings can now need water nearby: the Distillery and the Potter's Workshop only work with a well, the Town Hall or open water within 12 cells. The inspector says whether they have it.
- Silver and gold: a new Mine upgrade, Deep Veins (after Deep Shafts), lets miners dig silver (8 hours a unit) and gold (16), each up to a cap you set (defaults 10 and 6) after the other ores and before stone. They are only trade goods, worth a great deal (gold 80, silver 30), and merchants bring and ask for them.
- Four kinds of merchant, each with its own goods and cart colours: grain merchants (food, seeds and saplings, sometimes animals), metal traders (iron, coal, tools, silver and gold), herders (wool, leather, meat, eggs, and animals) and fine goods caravans (honey, spirits, pottery, fine clothes, precious metals). They call equally often in every season, and nobody asks for what they are selling.
- Luxuries (honey, spirits, pottery, fine clothes, silver, gold) fetch 1.4 to 2.2 times their value from any merchant who asks for them.
- Seasonal prices: grain and fruit are dear in spring and cheap at harvest, firewood, coal, clothes and spirits dear in winter, and so on. What is on offer does not change; only the price, fixed for the length of a visit. The barter window marks goods [dear] or [cheap].
- The Ledger has a Trade graph and a Trade tab: totals, every deal (who, what, the value each way) and every visit.
- An empty pasture now has room for any animal a merchant offers, and takes up the animal led to it. Animals waiting at the Trading Post with no pasture room are warned of in the log and the inspector.
- The Trading Post can keep honey and spirits back for trade: citizens only drink what is over the level you set.
- Every merchant also brings a small amount of one off-line item (firewood, manure, anything tradeable).
- A note under the top bar shows a merchant on the way, here (with the days left) or leaving; a warning in the log when a merchant is about to leave with an order unfinished; the barter window shows the usual price beside a dear or cheap one and is wider.
- Greener, deeper grass and calmer sand and spring oak leaves, and slightly softer daytime light, so the terrain looks less washed out.
